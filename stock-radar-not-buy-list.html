Stock Radar Scores Are Not Buy Lists

Audience: English readers learning how research radars rank equity candidates
Grounded on: alphagbm.com/en · alphagbm.com/en/stock · pricing
Publisher: Greeks Lab (greeks-lab) — educational notes about public AlphaGBM product language
Status: Publish-ready · 2026-10-26 (Asia/Shanghai)

Short answer

A stock radar on a research workbench is a filtered, dated shortlist of equity candidates—not a whole-market ranking and not a buy list. On AlphaGBM’s public English homepage, stock radar shows up to nine candidates that pass data and profitability checks, without fillers. A score of ≥80 marks a high score observation, not a buy signal. Quotes and scores keep their own data dates. Treat the radar as a queue of what to investigate next through fundamentals, sentiment, and risk—not as an order ticket.

What the public product language actually says

From AlphaGBM’s English homepage (Stock radar section):

  • Only candidates that pass data and profitability checks are shown; missing coverage is not filled with examples.
  • Up to 9 candidates; not a whole-market ranking.
  • ≥80 = high score mark, not a buy signal.
  • “Sharp moves” mean an absolute change ≥5% over the quoted interval—not a verified cause.
  • Quotes and scores retain their own data dates.
  • Scores and yield data are for information and research only—not investment advice or a guarantee of returns. Past performance does not predict future results.

The stock research surface (/en/stock) frames the product as researching stocks through fundamentals, sentiment, and risk, with a free daily research allowance shared across stocks and options (see Pricing for live plan language). AlphaGBM describes itself as an AI multi-asset investment research workbench—research tools, agent workflows, and Skills—not a broker that places orders for you.

How to read a radar row without over-claiming

Phrase on the page Safer reading Common misuse
Up to 9 candidates, no fillers A short screen under stated checks “Top 9 stocks in the market”
≥80 high score Relative high mark inside that screen “Buy now” / guaranteed edge
Sharp move ≥5% Size of a quoted interval move Proven catalyst or narrative
Dated quotes / scores Snapshot with its own timestamp Live tick-by-tick advice
Fundamentals · Sentiment · Risk Lenses for evidence Single-number trading rule

Practical checklist before you deepen research:

  1. Note the data dates on quotes and scores—do not assume they share one clock.
  2. Separate price move size from cause; a ≥5% label is not a verified thesis.
  3. Pull supporting and opposing evidence across fundamentals, sentiment, and risk, instead of acting on the score alone.
  4. Remember free vs paid language is about research credits / allowance, not trading access—AlphaGBM is not a broker.
